# Code and figures accompanying

## *Well-posedness, Stability, and Bifurcation in an Acid-Mediated Tumour Invasion Model with Interspecific Competition*

Yaya Youssouf Yaya & Barka Ibrahim Mahamat — revised submission to *Bulletin of Mathematical Biology*, manuscript BMAB-D-26-00386.

---

Every figure and every numerical claim in the paper is regenerated by the scripts below. No figure hard-codes a parameter value: `params.py` is the single source of truth, and the random seed is fixed to 42.

The nine figures of the manuscript, as submitted, are in `figures/`, so that a regenerated file can be compared against the published one; see the table below for their numbers in each revision.

`supplementary.pdf` is the Supplementary Material of the manuscript, included here because it is where the analytical statements the code checks are written out: the nondimensionalisation, the coefficient-by-coefficient expansion behind the spatial-stability proposition, the Volterra functional and its optimal weights, the threshold set of the spatial problem, the codimension-two unfolding, and the section-by-section index of what `sympy_verification.py` verifies. It is the version submitted with the first revision, not the published one.

## Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| **`params.py`** | Single source of truth for the three phenotypes of Table 1 (indolent, moderate, aggressive), as a frozen dataclass, plus the critical threshold `delta_c`, the shared grid and time-step constants, and a checker for Hypothesis (HL). Imported by everything else. |
| **`main.py`** | The solver (`Parameters`, `Grid2D`, `Solver`) and most figure generators. The scheme is IMEX: five-point Laplacian with homogeneous Neumann conditions imposed by ghost nodes, assembled as a Kronecker sum; backward-Euler diffusion with sparse LU factorisations computed once and reused; explicit reaction. |
| **`bifurcation_plane.py`** | The (α, δ/δ_c) phase portrait and the two transcritical bifurcation diagrams (Fig. 1). Purely analytic: it plots the closed-form equilibrium expressions, with no simulation involved. |
| **`bistability.py`** | The bistable regime (Fig. 7): two runs with *identical* parameters, differing only in the radius of the initial tumour seed, converging to opposite equilibria. Also reports the reflection asymmetry of each run, the kill time and the receding fraction. |
| **`critical_radius.py`** | The measurement campaign behind the size threshold: bisection of the critical seed radius across box sizes, diffusivity ratios, acid clearance rates, seed shapes, seed densities and seed positions. Its results are cached in `critical_radius_results.json`, which ships with the Zenodo archive; with that file in the working directory, `--report-only` re-tabulates the whole campaign without re-running anything. |
| **`fisher_kpp.py`** | The travelling-front speed (Fig. 8), measured on a fine mesh over successive late time windows. See the note below. |
| **`bistability_sweep.py`** | A coarse mesh-independence check on the bistability threshold, kept as the historical entry point that preceded the bisection campaign. |
| **`supplementary.pdf`** | The Supplementary Material of the manuscript (14 pp.), which states the results the scripts verify and indexes the symbolic checks by section. |
| **`sympy_verification.py`** | Symbolic verification of every algebraic claim in the paper: the Routh–Hurwitz coefficients at the coexistence equilibrium; the Sylvester minors that yield Hypothesis (HL) and the closed form of its optimal weights; the Sotomayor coefficients on both transcritical curves and the invariance of the branch slope; the closed-form Routh–Hurwitz identity valid for *every* spatial mode, together with the vanishing of all three two-cycle products of the Jacobian; the two-branch admissibility of the coexistence equilibrium with its saddle classification; the segment of equilibria at the codimension-two point and its reduced scalar flow; the quasi-static Bessel profile of the acid; and the absence of Turing instability by cooperativity, sampled over 20 000 random parameter draws. All residuals are exactly zero. |

## How to run

```bash
python3 params.py               # prints the three phenotypes and checks (HL)
python3 sympy_verification.py   # symbolic proof of every algebraic claim (~1 min)
python3 bifurcation_plane.py    # -> bifurcation_plane.pdf     (Fig. 1)   (~2 s)
python3 main.py                 # -> Figs. 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9               (~15 min)
python3 bistability.py          # -> bistability.pdf           (Fig. 7)   (~5 min)
python3 fisher_kpp.py           # -> fisher_kpp_speed.pdf      (Fig. 8)   (~10 min)
python3 critical_radius.py --report-only    # re-tabulates the cached campaign
```

Each run prints a short report — parameter values, observed convergence orders, front-speed ratios, the violation report — so that the printed numbers can be matched against those quoted in the manuscript.

The measurement campaign itself is expensive and is therefore shipped with its results:

```bash
python3 critical_radius.py --report-only          # instant, needs the cached JSON
python3 critical_radius.py --all --workers 4      # re-runs everything (many hours)
python3 critical_radius.py domains screening      # re-runs two experiments only
```

## Three notes on correctness

**The invariant region is not enforced.** The solver runs with `enforce_clip=False` by default. Clipping the iterates into the admissible box would conceal whether the scheme actually preserves it, so instead every step records the worst pointwise violation of each of the six bounds, and `violation_report()` returns the maximum over the run. Across every simulation in the paper the worst excess stays at the level of floating-point round-off. This is the discrete counterpart of Theorem 3.1, and it is a prediction being tested, not a constraint being imposed.

**The convergence study uses nested grids.** Grids are of the form `2^k + 1` (51, 101, 201, 401, 801) so that the coarse nodes are *exactly* a subset of the fine ones. This matters. The more natural-looking sequence 50, 100, 200 has 49, 99, 199 intervals, which do **not** nest, so sub-sampling the fine grid every other node compares the two fields at physically different locations — a silent error that contaminated an earlier version of this study. An assertion in `fig_convergence` now prevents it from recurring.

**The Neumann stencil carries a factor two at the boundary.** The nodes lie *on* the boundary, so the homogeneous Neumann condition sets the ghost value `u_{-1}` equal to `u_1` and the first row of the Laplacian reads `2(u_1 - u_0)/h^2`. The halved form, which suits a cell-centred grid and not this one, leaves an `O(1)` truncation error along the whole boundary and degrades the scheme to first order. `main.py` keeps that failure mode reachable behind the `BOUNDARY_HALVED` flag, and `python main.py --boundary-control` measures both and writes the numbers to `values_boundary_control.tex`. Until the second revision the flag halved only the off-diagonal entry and so built `(u_1 - 2u_0)/h^2`, a Dirichlet-like row, not the halved Neumann row the text describes; the figures printed in the first revision (orders 0.947 and 0.988) were those of that row. A referee spotted it. `test_boundary_rows.py` (exit code 0/1) now pins down the first row of both variants, and fails on the old code. With the halved row as described, the observed orders are 1.114 and 1.029 against 1.969 and 1.994 for the ghost-node row, errors about eight times smaller than with the old row; the conclusion, first order, stands.

## On the Fisher–KPP front speed

A fit on an early window returns `c_num / c* ≈ 0.93`, which is impossible for a front issued from compactly supported data, since comparison already gives `c ≥ c*`. The artefact is the window, not the mesh. Refining the mesh on a *fixed* early window leaves the ratio at `0.9322`, `0.9300`, `0.9297` and `0.9296` for `Nx = 201`, `401`, `801` and `1601` — from three to twenty-three nodes across the wing of the front — so the measurement converges without approaching 1. What removes the deficit is a later window: a pulled KPP front approaches its asymptotic speed only algebraically slowly, by the Bramson correction `x_f(t) = c* t - (3 / 2 λ*) ln t + O(1)`, so *any* early fit under-estimates `c*` by an amount decaying like `1/t`.

```bash
python3 fisher_kpp.py --meshsweep  # the fixed-window mesh sweep above  (~25 min)
python3 fisher_kpp.py --refine     # mesh and time-step control          (~20 min)
python3 fisher_kpp.py --longtime   # t = 250 on [0,42]                   (~80 min)
python3 fisher_kpp.py --edge       # leading-edge structure              (~6 min)
```

Measured on late windows with an adequately resolved mesh the ratio rises monotonically and crosses 1 from below, as it must, and the small final excess above `c*` is physical rather than numerical. `--longtime` settles that it is not a slowly decaying transient: carried to `t = 250` on a domain lengthened to `[0,42]`, with `h` and `dt` unchanged, the ratio rises and saturates at `1.0053` (`1.0037, 1.0046, 1.0050, 1.0052, 1.0053, 1.0053` on the six windows from `[55,75]` to `[210,250]`). The first of these reproduces the value obtained on the shorter domain, so the excess is insensitive to the domain length as well as to the mesh. The front is therefore weakly **pushed**, by `0.53 %`.

`--edge` measures why. The acid diffuses two hundred times faster than the cells here (diffusion coefficient `1` against `delta1 = delta2 = 5e-3` in nondimensional units), reaches ahead of the front and depresses the healthy tissue, so the per-capita growth `beta(1 - T - alpha N)` exceeds its nominal value `beta(1 - alpha)` exactly where `T < alpha (1 - N)`, which at `t = 60` is the whole forward wing `T < 0.07` — by `5.2 %` at the level `T = 1e-2`, `4.0 %` at `1e-3` and `1.1 %` at `1e-6`, decaying to zero as `T -> 0` and `N -> 1`. Because the excess vanishes at the leading edge it leaves the linear spreading speed untouched; because it is present across the wing it makes the nonlinear front outrun its own linearisation. `--kpp-set` recomputes these figures from the saved profile and writes them to `values_kpp_violation.tex`. (The first revision quoted `beta(1 - alpha N)`, without the self-limitation term, and stated that the KPP condition fails wherever `N < 1`; both are corrected here.) Both runs save their data (`fisher_kpp_longtime.npz`, `fisher_kpp_edge.npz`) so the window analysis can be redone without repeating the integration.
